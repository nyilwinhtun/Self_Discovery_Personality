# InnerCompass — Cognitive Interview Guide (Internal)

## Purpose

Use this guide to understand how Burmese readers interpret each draft question. It is a qualitative wording and usability step. It is not a psychometric validation study.

## Suggested participants

For an initial round, invite a small, varied group of Burmese readers (for example, 6–10 people) with different age ranges, education backgrounds, and everyday contexts. This is a practical starting point for wording review, not a statistically representative sample. A qualified researcher should determine later pilot and validation sample sizes.

Participation must be voluntary. Explain that the questions are drafts, not diagnoses. Participants may skip any question, stop at any time, or decline to explain a personal answer.

## Interview method

Use think-aloud and paraphrasing. Do not lead the participant toward a preferred answer. Do not tell them what the question is “supposed” to mean before they explain it.

For each item, ask:

1. “ဒီမေးခွန်းကို ကိုယ့်စကားနဲ့ ပြန်ရှင်းပြပေးနိုင်မလား။”
2. “ဒီမေးခွန်းကို ဖတ်တဲ့အခါ ဘယ်လိုအခြေအနေမျိုးကို စဉ်းစားမိသလဲ။”
3. “နားလည်ရခက်တဲ့ စကားလုံး ဒါမှမဟုတ် စာပိုဒ် ရှိသလား။”
4. “ဒီအဖြေရွေးချယ်စရာ ၅ ခုထဲက ကိုယ့်အတွေ့အကြုံနဲ့ ကိုက်ညီတာကို ရွေးရတာ လွယ်သလား။”
5. “ဒီမေးခွန်းက ကိုယ့်ကို အပြစ်တင်သလို၊ အဖြေတစ်ခုကို ဦးတည်စေသလို ခံစားရသလား။”

Do not require the participant to reveal a private story. Ask about interpretation, not the details of a sensitive event.

## What the reviewer should record

For each item, record:

- **Comprehension:** correct / partly clear / unclear.
- **Recall:** easy to think of a typical example / needed prompting / no relevant example.
- **Judgement:** neutral / felt judgemental / socially desirable response pressure.
- **Response mapping:** easy to choose a scale point / two options felt similar / no suitable option.
- **Context:** any family, work, language, disability, safety, resource-access, or cultural assumption noticed.
- **Suggested wording:** participant's own simpler wording, if offered.

## Interviewer safeguards

- Never praise one answer over another.
- Do not explain that a question is reverse-keyed.
- Do not diagnose or interpret the participant's personality.
- Do not collect unnecessary names, contact details, trauma narratives, or sensitive personal histories.
- Store notes securely and anonymise them for analysis.
- If a participant becomes uncomfortable, offer to skip the item or stop the interview.

## Decision rules after interviews

Revise an item if multiple participants misunderstand its key phrase, interpret it as a different behaviour, cannot map their experience to the response scale, or report judgemental pressure. A single serious accessibility or safety concern is also sufficient to review an item.

After revision, give the item a new bank version. Never silently change wording for a participant with an active saved attempt.

## Important limitation

A successful cognitive interview round only suggests that the items are more understandable to those participants. It does not establish reliability, factor structure, norms, validity, or suitability for clinical or high-stakes decisions.
